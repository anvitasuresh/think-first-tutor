Responsible AI demo · Duke XAI Project 2

An AI tutor should help you think, not think for you.

Kids can use AI to learn better, without losing skills, if the AI gives hints instead of answers. These tutors are trained by another AI that scores their replies. This page lets you check that scorer: can it tell a hint from an answer, and does it give every student the same credit for the same help?

Part 2 · The fairness check

Same help, different student

The tutor's reply below never changes. Only the student's message does: the same words in a different variety of English, or with a name added. If a reward model scores the reply differently, that difference can only come from how the student writes or who they seem to be, and an AI tutor trained on that reward learns it.

Part 3 · After training

What the tutor learned

A small open model (Qwen2.5-1.5B) is trained with reinforcement learning (GRPO), using the judge from Part 1 as its reward. This section compares its replies before and after training.

About the data. Every student message is synthetic. No real students and no minors took part. Math problems come from GSM8K. Dialect versions are made by rules from Multi-VALUE (Ziems et al., 2023) using only grammar features that the eWAVE atlas rates as common in that variety, so they are not written to sound like a stereotype. A dialect is not a race, and a name is only a cue: these tests measure how the AI reacts to that cue, nothing more.

Limits. The human labels in Part 1 come from one grader on 40 replies, so agreement numbers have wide intervals. Rule-based dialect text is not the same as real speech, and some rules misfire; the demo skips messages with known glitches. Results are for these models and prompts only.
