You are grading one reply from a math tutor to a middle-school student. The tutor's job is to help the student learn, not to do the problem for them.

You will see the math problem, its correct final answer (for your reference only; the student cannot see it), the student's message, and the tutor's reply. Grade only the tutor's reply.

SCAFFOLDING: does the reply guide the student, or do the work for them? Decide in this order and stop at the first rule that fits.
  0 = Gives it away. Pick 0 if ANY of these is true:
      - the reply states a final answer, even a wrong one, even inside a "hint" or "check whether it comes out to ...";
      - the reply carries out all of the remaining calculations, so the student has nothing left to compute;
      - the reply states the result of the very step the student is stuck on (for example "so Kelsey has 43, now subtract").
      Friendly wording, a question at the end, or the word "hint" does not change this: judge what the reply actually reveals.
  1 = Neither. Pick 1 if the reply gives nothing away but still does not guide, or does most of the thinking:
      - it is generic or vague ("What do you think?", "Check your setup", "Try again!"), or off target;
      - it lays out the complete plan for every remaining step, so only arithmetic is left for the student;
      - it does all of the work except one trivial final operation;
      - it points the student in a wrong or confusing direction.
  2 = Guides. Pick 2 only if the reply gives a hint or asks a question about the student's specific next step, reveals no answer or intermediate result the student was meant to find, and leaves the key reasoning for that step to the student.
  Most replies that explain a lot are 0 or 1. Use 2 only when the student clearly still has real thinking to do.

HELPFULNESS: how much would this reply help this student make progress, given what they said?
  1 = No help, or wrong or misleading math (including a wrong answer or a wrong step).
  2 = Little help: vague, or mostly ignores what the student said.
  3 = Some help: correct and relevant, but unclear or not tailored to where the student is stuck. A correct worked solution is usually 3.
  4 = Helpful: correct, clear, and addresses the student's specific situation.
  5 = Very helpful: all of 4, and also checks the student's understanding or builds on their own work.
Helpfulness is separate from scaffolding: a reply that gives away the answer can still be clear and correct.

Respond in exactly this format and nothing else:
SCAFFOLDING: <0, 1 or 2>
HELPFULNESS: <1, 2, 3, 4 or 5>
